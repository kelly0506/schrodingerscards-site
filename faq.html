Requests

Using the request form. 7 questions
How does the request process work?
  1. You send the request. Nothing is set aside yet.
  2. We reply within 48 hours and tell you what is actually still available.
  3. We agree on a pickup date. Change it, ask questions, adjust the list. That is what the reply is for.
  4. You have 72 hours from that date to pay and collect. Need longer? Just tell us and we will add 24 hours.
  5. Come to Booth 151. Give your name and the booth number. Your items will be waiting inside our case, ready to pay for.
Does sending a request reserve my items?

No. A request tells us what you are after. It does not hold anything and it does not guarantee we can fill it.

We sell at a physical booth, so stock moves without warning and a fair amount of what we carry is down to the last one or two. Nothing is actually set aside until we have replied and agreed a pickup date with you.

How quickly will you get back to me?

Within 48 hours. We will confirm what is still on the shelf, flag anything that has sold, and suggest a pickup window.

If you have not heard from us in two days, something went wrong on our end. Email us again and we will sort it out.

Why can I only request six items?

We are a small family-run booth, not a warehouse. Six keeps requests to a size we can actually pull, set aside, and hold for you without taking the shelf apart.

If you want more than that, say so in the notes and we will work something out.

What counts as one item?

Most things count as one each. A booster box is one item, a gift set is one item, an English booster pack is one item.

Slim boosters are the exception. They are the small Chinese packs, four or five cards each, and we stock plenty of them. Up to five slim boosters of the same set count as one item.

The allowance is per set, not across your whole request. Five Chasing Glory is one item. Three Chasing Glory and two Stellar Crystal is two items, because those are two different things for us to pull and bag.

The request page does the math for you and shows a running total.

Can I change or cancel my request after sending it?

Yes, and there is no penalty for it. Just reply to our email. Adding, removing, swapping, or calling the whole thing off is all fine.

We would much rather you tell us than leave items sitting in the case.

Can I ask about something that is not on the list?

Please do. The request form has three boxes for exactly that: specific singles, graded cards, and products not listed.

Our booth stock is wider than the request page, which only covers sealed Pokémon. We also work with a distributor, so if it is something we can order in, we will tell you.

Pickup & Holding

Collecting at the booth. 5 questions
How long will you hold my items?

72 hours from the pickup date we agree on. Not from when you send the request, from the date we settle on together in the reply.

Need a bit longer? Tell us before the window closes and we will add 24 hours. We cannot promise to hold things past that without hearing from you, because we only have one of most of them and other people are asking.

What if I miss the pickup window?

The items go back out on the shelf, and you have not lost anything. You paid nothing, so there is nothing to refund and no hard feelings.

Message us and we will check whether it is still there. Often it is.

How do I actually collect at the booth?

Go to Booth 151 and tell the front counter your name and the booth number. Your items will be waiting inside our case.

You do not need to print anything or show the email, though having it on your phone never hurts.

Do you ship?

Not through the request form. Everything on that page is pickup only at Treasure Cove Mall in Jacksonville, Texas.

If you are not local, our eBay store ships nationwide. That is also where our Magic: The Gathering goes, since none of it is on the request page.

Can someone else pick up for me?

Yes, anyone can collect it. We do not need to know in advance who is coming.

One thing matters: they must give the name of the person who placed the order. That is what we write on the tag and what the front counter matches against. Send a friend who gives their own name and nothing will line up.

Payment

How and when you pay. 4 questions
When do I pay?

In person, at the booth, when you collect. Never before.

Can I pay in advance to make sure I get it?

No. We do not offer prepayment and we will never ask you for card details by email.

Two reasons. It protects you, because you are not sending money to a booth you have not stood in front of. And it protects us from having to chase refunds when something sells before you arrive.

If anyone claiming to be us asks you to pay up front, it is not us.

What payment methods do you take?

Cash and all major credit cards.

Payment is taken at Treasure Cove's front counter rather than by us directly, so the mall's terms apply.

Are prices on the request page?

No, deliberately. Sealed prices move with the market and we would rather quote you something accurate in our reply than leave a stale number on a page.

Ask and we will tell you. There is no haggling game here.

What We Carry

Stock, formats, and what the request page covers. 4 questions
What do you actually stock?

Pokémon is the core of it: sealed product in English and Simplified Chinese, singles from a dollar upward, vintage from the Base Set era onward, and graded slabs.

Alongside that we carry Magic: The Gathering singles and sealed, plus sports cards and a smaller amount of Yu-Gi-Oh and other games. There is a fuller breakdown under what we carry.

The request page is sealed Pokémon only. It is the part of our stock that is stable enough to list.

Why is there no Magic: The Gathering on the request page?

Our Magic stock is mostly singles, which move far too quickly to keep a list accurate.

For Magic, either browse the case in person or check our eBay store, which ships nationwide. You can also use the "products not listed" box on the request form and tell us what you are hunting.

What is the difference between a slim booster and a jumbo booster?

It is a Chinese format thing, and it trips people up constantly.

  • Slim boosters hold four or five cards. Roughly half the thickness of an English pack. Boxes of these run 15 to 18 packs.
  • Jumbo boosters hold 20 to 25 cards in a single pack. Boxes run six packs, so a jumbo box is fewer, fatter openings.

For comparison, an English booster pack is 10 cards. Every listing on the request page spells out how many boosters and how many cards per booster.

What is a Gem Box?

Gem Box Volumes are Chinese all-foil reprint sets. Every card in every pack is a shiny, which makes them a very different opening experience from a normal booster where most of the pack is filler.

They come 18 packs to a box, four cards per pack. We usually have Gem Box Volume 4, 5 and 6 around in some combination.

Chinese Product

The part people ask about most. 4 questions
Are the Chinese cards real Pokémon cards?

Yes. These are official Pokémon releases printed for the Chinese market, not counterfeits and not proxies. They carry the same licensing as any other Pokémon card, just in a different language and distribution region.

They are not officially distributed in the United States, which is exactly why they are interesting and why you do not see them in big box stores.

How are they different from English cards?

The card stock feels different, the foiling is noticeably brighter, and the set structure does not map one-to-one onto English releases. Chinese sets often recombine cards from several English sets, and some have artwork that never got an English printing at all.

Print runs are also much smaller, which is part of the appeal for collectors.

Can I play with them in a tournament?

Treat them as collector and casual play cards unless you have checked. Event rules vary by organizer and by format, and some require cards in the local language or ask you to carry a translation for anything that is not.

Ask your tournament organizer before you build around them. We would rather tell you that than sell you a deck you cannot register.

Why should I buy Chinese product instead of English?

Honestly, sometimes you should not. If you are chasing a specific English card, buy English.

Where Chinese product wins is cards per dollar and novelty. A jumbo pack gives you 25 cards in one opening. Gem Box boxes are all foil. And the artwork variants genuinely are things your friends will not have seen. It is a different kind of fun, not a cheaper version of the same one.

Singles & Graded

Everything the request page does not cover. 4 questions
Can I ask you to find a specific single?

Yes. Use the "specific singles" box on the request form. Give us the card name, the set, and the collector number if you have it, since a lot of cards share a name across a dozen printings.

We will check the case first, then tell you honestly whether it is something we can source.

Do you carry graded cards?

Yes, slabs rotate through the case regularly. They are not listed on the request page because the stock turns over too fast to keep accurate.

Use the "graded cards" box on the request form and tell us what you are after, including any grade or grading company you prefer.

Do you grade cards?

No, and no card shop can. Grading is done by professional third-party companies who authenticate the card, assign a numeric grade, and seal it in a tamper-evident slab. The main ones are PSA, CGC and Beckett. You submit to them directly.

What we can do is tell you whether a card looks worth submitting at all. That is a cheaper question and usually the more useful one, because grading costs money per card plus shipping both ways, and on most cards it costs more than the grade adds.

We sell graded cards. We just do not grade them, and you should be wary of anyone who tells you they do it in-house.

I am new to this. Where do I start?

Come stand at the case. Genuinely, that is the answer. Tell us what you like and what you want to spend, and we will point you at something rather than upsell you.

If you want to read first, our collection guide walks through how sets and rarities work without assuming you already know.

Selling & Collections

Bringing cards to us, and finding out what you have. 8 questions
Do you buy cards and collections?

Yes. Singles, sealed product, bulk, and whole collections. A shoebox someone found in a closet is genuinely one of our favorite things to be handed.

Start with the contact form rather than the request form, and tell us roughly what you have. Photos help more than descriptions. We will reply and set up a time to meet.

Can I just bring my collection to the booth?

Please arrange a time with us first. Treasure Cove staffs the front counter for every vendor, so the mall is open far more often than we are actually there. Turning up with a box unannounced usually means nobody is around who can look at it, and the counter staff cannot value cards or make you an offer.

Send the contact form with rough photos and we will agree a time to meet. It is worth the day or two that takes: you get our full attention and an unhurried look, rather than whatever we can manage between other things.

I inherited a box of cards and have no idea what any of it is. Can you help?

Yes, and you do not need to know anything first. Send photos, or arrange a time to bring it in, and we will walk you through what is in there.

We will also tell you when the answer is "not much". Most collections from the late nineties are worth far less than people hope, and a few are worth far more. You deserve an accurate number and we will do our best to get you one.

What should I do before bringing a collection in?

Mostly nothing, and that is deliberate. The common instinct is to tidy things up, and tidying is what usually destroys value.

  • Do not clean or wipe the cards. Even a soft cloth leaves micro-scratches on foil, and a wiped card can drop a full grade.
  • Do not trim, flatten, or press anything. A trimmed card is worth close to nothing and is treated as altered.
  • Do not throw away boxes, wrappers, or packaging. Sometimes the packaging is the valuable part.
  • Leave cards in whatever sleeves or binders they are already in. Moving them around is where corners get dinged.
  • If you want to sort, sort by set, not by what looks shiny. Shiny is a poor predictor of value.
How do you work out what a collection is worth?

Condition and scarcity do most of the work, and the card's name does less than people expect. The same card can be worth a dollar or several hundred depending on centering, edges, surface, and whether it is an early print or a later reprint.

We price against recently completed sales, not asking prices. What somebody is asking for a card online is not evidence of anything. We are happy to show you what we are looking at.

Will you buy the whole collection or just the good bits?

Usually the whole lot, and we will say up front if that is not the case. Cherry-picking the best cards leaves you holding the part that is hardest to sell, which is not much of a favor.

If there is something in there we are not the right buyer for, we will tell you where you would do better.

Do you charge to look at a collection?

No. Ask and we will tell you what we think, whether or not you end up selling to us. Just arrange a time with us first rather than turning up with it.

Should I sell to you, or sell it myself?

We are the buyer, so we are not a neutral party here and we will not pretend otherwise.

What we will do is tell you what we see and what it has been selling for. Our collection guide covers how condition and rarity drive value, which is the part that decides this. Selling to a shop is faster, certain, and pays less than a good private sale. Selling it yourself pays more and costs you time, fees, and the risk of a bad buyer. For a big single card you will often do better listing it yourself, and we will say so.
